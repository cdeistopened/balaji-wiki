# Popup-to-Permanent Planner

Given an internet community that wants to materialize physically, plan the evolution from a one-time popup gathering to a recurring event to a permanent settlement. Use the Zuzalu model and Balaji's "popups are the new startups" framework.

## Background: Popups as the New Startups

Balaji argues that the 2020s equivalent of founding a startup is founding a popup: an internet community that temporarily meets in the physical world.

> "An internet community that temporarily meets up in the physical world, and terraforms it in some fashion."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, "Popups are the New Startups" (Oct 2025)

The progression: internet companies proved that online communities could create economic value. Internet currencies proved that online communities could create financial systems. Internet communities materializing physically (popups) prove they can create places.

> "Begin with a popup, and get your online friends in the same place at the same time."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, "Popups are the New Startups" (Oct 2025)

The 2025 Network State Conference demonstrated the scale: crypto executives (Vitalik Buterin, Brian Armstrong), government representatives (Singapore, El Salvador, UAE), and venture capitalists gathering to discuss startup societies and charter cities. This is no longer theoretical.

## The Consultation

### Step 1: Understand the Community

Ask these questions:

1. What online community do you want to bring together physically? How many active members?
2. What's the shared identity or mission? (If they have a one commandment, note it. If not, identify the implicit one.)
3. Has the community ever met in person before? If so, what happened?
4. What's the geographic distribution of members? Any concentration areas?
5. What's your budget and timeline for the first gathering?

### Step 2: Classify the Popup Type

Not all popups are the same. Identify which type fits:

| Type | Duration | Scale | Infrastructure | Example |
|------|----------|-------|---------------|---------|
| **Meetup** | Hours | 10-50 | Rented venue | Monthly community dinner |
| **Popup Weekend** | 2-3 days | 50-200 | Hotel/retreat center | Weekend retreat or hackathon |
| **Popup Week** | 5-10 days | 100-500 | Rented campus or hotel block | Conference + co-living |
| **Popup Month** | 30-90 days | 200-2,000 | Village or town rental | Zuzalu (2 months, Montenegro) |
| **Popup Season** | 3-6 months | 500-5,000 | Semi-permanent campus | Network School fellowship |

The right type depends on community size, budget, and how much "terraforming" is desired. Start smaller than you think you should. A great popup weekend beats a mediocre popup month.

### Step 3: Design the First Popup

**Location Selection Criteria:**
1. **Visa accessibility**: Can most members actually get there? (International communities need visa-friendly jurisdictions)
2. **Cost**: Accommodation, venue, food for the duration at the target scale
3. **Internet**: Non-negotiable for a digital community materializing physically
4. **Walkability**: Members should be able to run into each other organically (this is where the magic happens)
5. **Government friendliness**: Is the local government open to experimental communities? (Montenegro, Portugal, Costa Rica, Thailand have track records)

**Programming Design:**
The popup should NOT feel like a conference. It should feel like living in a community. Key ratio:

- 30% structured (talks, workshops, working sessions)
- 30% semi-structured (meals together, organized activities, office hours)
- 40% unstructured (free time, serendipitous encounters, organic collaboration)

> "1000+ friendly internet communities around the world that anyone can choose from."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, "Popups are the New Startups" (Oct 2025)

**Application Process:**
Select attendees like you'd select early employees, not conference attendees. Balaji's Network School model: Form -> Zoom -> Room -> Boom (application, interview, acceptance, arrival). 4,000 applications for 128 slots at Network School demonstrates that selectivity creates value.

### Step 4: Plan the Progression

Each successful popup should create the conditions for the next, larger iteration. Map the 5-step progression:

**Step A: First Popup (Proof of Concept)**
- Goal: Get 50-200 people in one place for 2-7 days
- Success metric: 80%+ would come back. Organic projects started during the event.
- Output: Photos, documentation, shared memories that strengthen online community

**Step B: Recurring Popup (Habit Formation)**
- Goal: Run the popup annually or semi-annually
- Success metric: Waiting list forms. Returning attendees bring friends.
- Output: Traditions, inside culture, a physical identity to match the digital one

**Step C: Multi-Site Popups (Network Effect)**
- Goal: Community members organize satellite popups in multiple cities
- Success metric: Popups happen without the founder organizing them
- Output: Geographic nodes that could become permanent

**Step D: Semi-Permanent Presence (Infrastructure)**
- Goal: Lease or purchase property for community use between popups
- Success metric: Community members move near the property. Year-round activity.
- Output: The first node of the network archipelago

**Step E: Permanent Settlement (Network Archipelago)**
- Goal: Multiple properties in multiple locations, connected digitally
- Success metric: Members can live full-time in community-owned spaces
- Output: A functioning physical community with an on-chain census

### Step 5: Design the Economics

Popups need sustainable economics to become permanent:

**Popup Funding Models:**
- Ticket sales (straightforward but limits accessibility)
- Sponsorship (works for tech-adjacent communities)
- Token-gated (community token required for attendance)
- Hybrid: subsidized tickets + sponsor funding + community treasury

**Progression to Permanent Economics:**
- Popup: Per-event ticket revenue
- Recurring popup: Annual membership + event fees
- Semi-permanent: Membership dues + property rental income
- Permanent: Community GDP (internal economy, property appreciation, service fees)

### Step 6: Avoid Common Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Description | Prevention |
|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Conference trap** | It feels like a conference, not a community | 40% unstructured time, co-living over hotel rooms |
| **Tourist trap** | People come once for novelty, never return | Selectivity in admission, projects that continue after the popup |
| **Founder dependency** | Nothing happens without the founder present | Empower regional leaders by Step C |
| **Scale too fast** | Jump from 50 to 5,000 without intermediate steps | Each popup should be 2-3x the previous, not 10x |
| **No digital bridge** | The physical event doesn't strengthen the online community | Document everything, create shared digital artifacts, maintain communication channels |
| **Financial unsustainability** | Each popup loses money | Design economics before scale; if the first popup can't break even, the tenth won't either |
