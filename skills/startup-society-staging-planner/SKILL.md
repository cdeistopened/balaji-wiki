# Startup Society Staging Planner

Identify where a community sits on the startup-society-to-network-state progression. Prescribe the specific milestones needed to advance to the next stage. Use Balaji's explicit analogy to startup funding rounds to make the abstract concrete.

## Background: The Staging Model

Balaji maps community building to startup building. Just as you don't found a public company directly, you don't found a network state directly. There are waypoints.

> "You found a startup society and hope to scale it into a network state that achieves diplomatic recognition from a pre-existing government, just as you don't found a public company directly, but instead found a startup company and hope to scale it into a public company that achieves 'diplomatic recognition' from a pre-existing exchange like the NASDAQ."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, Ch 5.3

The staging:

| Community Stage | Startup Analogy | Key Capability |
|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Startup Society** | Pre-seed / Idea stage | Online community with a moral premise, no collective action yet |
| **Network Union** | Seed / Series A | Digital community capable of collective action |
| **Network Archipelago** | Series B / Growth | Collective action manifested physically via crowdfunded properties connected by internet |
| **Network State** | IPO / Public Company | Diplomatic recognition from an existing government |

## The Consultation

### Step 1: Diagnose Current Stage

Ask these questions:

1. Describe your community in one paragraph. How many members? Where do they gather?
2. What is your one commandment or moral premise? (If none, route to `one-commandment-generator`.)
3. Has your community ever done anything together beyond talking? (Fundraised, built something, organized an event, collectively defended a member?)
4. Does your community own or lease any physical space?
5. Has any government entity acknowledged your community's existence?

**Stage Classification:**

**Pre-Startup Society** (no clear moral premise, just a social group):
- They have a community but no differentiation from mainstream society
- Missing: the one commandment
- Action: Define the moral innovation before anything else

**Startup Society** (moral premise + online community, no collective action):
- They have a reason to exist and people who believe in it
- Missing: the ability to act as a group
- Test: "Can you get 100 members to do the same thing on the same day?"

> "A startup society is a new community built internet-first, premised on a societal critique of its parent community, and founded for the purpose of addressing that specific societal problem in an opt-in way."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, Ch 2.9

**Network Union** (collective action capacity):
- They can coordinate, fundraise, defend members, build things together
- Missing: physical presence
- Test: "Can you get members to invest real money into shared physical infrastructure?"

> "Turning a startup society into a network union makes it a digital community capable of collective action."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, Ch 5.3

**Network Archipelago** (physical properties connected via internet):
- They own or lease real estate in multiple locations
- Members can physically gather in community-owned spaces
- Missing: diplomatic recognition
- Test: "Would an existing government take a meeting with your leadership?"

> "Turning that network union into a network archipelago manifests that collective action in the real world, as the community crowdfunds physical properties around the world and connects them via the internet."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, Ch 5.3

**Network State** (diplomatically recognized):
- An existing government has formally recognized your community's governance
- This is the endgame, equivalent to IPO

### Step 2: Define the Stage Transition

For the transition from current stage to next stage, identify:

**Startup Society -> Network Union:**
- Minimum viable collective action: What is one thing 100+ members could do together this month?
- Leadership formalization: Does the community have a recognized founder with clear authority?
- Communication infrastructure: Is there a "team dashboard" (not just a chat room)?
- Economic coordination: Can members pool resources (token, treasury, dues)?

**Network Union -> Network Archipelago:**
- First property: What physical space could the community crowdfund first? (Co-working space, apartment building, event venue)
- Geographic strategy: Where in the world are members concentrated? Where is regulation friendly?
- The popup test: Has the community done a Zuzalu-style temporary gathering? (See `references/frameworks/zuzalu-and-popups.md`)
- Digital-physical bridge: How will physical spaces be connected to the digital community? (ENS logins, smart locks, AR overlays)

**Network Archipelago -> Network State:**
- Census credibility: Can you cryptographically prove your population, income, and real estate footprint?
- Diplomatic pathway: Which existing governments are most likely to recognize you? (Small, innovation-friendly nations like Singapore, Estonia, El Salvador, UAE)
- Legal structure: What legal entity holds the properties? How is governance formalized?
- Scale threshold: Balaji doesn't specify an exact number, but the analogy to startups suggests you need significant "revenue" (GDP) and "users" (population) before any "exchange" (government) will list you

### Step 3: Identify Stage-Appropriate Activities

Many communities fail by doing activities appropriate to a later stage. A startup society trying to write a constitution is like a pre-seed startup writing an S-1.

| Stage | Appropriate Activities | Premature Activities |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Startup Society | Articulating the one commandment, recruiting believers, creating content, building the narrative | Buying land, designing governance, issuing tokens |
| Network Union | Collective fundraising, mutual aid, collective defense of members, shared projects | Physical real estate, diplomatic outreach, building a virtual capital |
| Network Archipelago | Crowdfunding properties, connecting them digitally, building on-chain census, hosting popups | Seeking diplomatic recognition before proving scale |
| Network State | Diplomatic outreach, formal governance, physical law enforcement | N/A (you're at the final stage) |

### Step 4: Apply the Missionary vs. Mercenary Test

At every stage, check whether members are missionaries or mercenaries.

> "The historian Paul Johnson once pointed out that the for-profit colonies in America failed but the religious ones had the cohesion and commitment to make it through the brutal winters."
> -- Balaji Srinivasan, The Network State, Ch 5.3

Warning signs of mercenary dominance:
- Members joined primarily for financial upside (token appreciation)
- No one can articulate the one commandment from memory
- Collective action only happens when there's a direct financial incentive
- Member retention drops when token price drops

## Disclaimer

This is an analytical framework based on Balaji Srinivasan's published thinking. Community building involves legal, financial, and social risks. The network state progression is a theoretical model; no community has completed the full journey from startup society to diplomatically recognized network state. Stage assessments are directional, not definitive.
